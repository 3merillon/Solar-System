Landmark field guide

English Wikipedia contributors wrote the featured article excerpts in
landmarks/articles/. Each record identifies the title and exact source revision.
Open https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid= followed by that revision number
for the article and its contributor history. These excerpts are licensed under
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/.
The excerpts are shortened and converted to plain text. The Gazetteer-to-article
identifiers come from Wikidata (CC0).

Lunar rim-to-floor measurements are from Rachael Hoover, A Lunar Crater Depth
Database for Craters >1km Diameter, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21500453
(CC BY 4.0). The guide matches a subset to IAU names by center and diameter
and rounds digital terrain model depths for display.

Venus crater observations are adapted from Robert Herrick's Venus Crater
Database, https://www.lpi.usra.edu/resources/vc/ (CC BY 4.0). The guide selects
named craters and describes their radar morphology. See Herrick, Sharpton,
Malin, Lyons and Feely (1997), "Morphology and Morphometry of Impact Craters,"
in Venus II (University of Arizona Press). Both survey licenses are
available at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/.

Local map-unit identifications are based on Tanaka et al. (2014), Geologic Map
of Mars, USGS SIM 3292, https://pubs.usgs.gov/sim/3292/, and Fortezzo et al.
(2020), Unified Geologic Map of the Moon, USGS Astrogeology. Each lookup uses
the Gazetteer center of a feature and the unit polygon on the regional map.
Venus regional elevations use the USGS Magellan Global Topography v02 grid,
with heights relative to a 6,051 km radius.
https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/venus_magellan_global_topography_4641m
